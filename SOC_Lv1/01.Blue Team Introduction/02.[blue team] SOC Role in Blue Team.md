# 51.SAL1 — SOC Role in Blue Team — Cheat Sheet

**Focus:** Understanding the Security Hierarchy, the specific roles within a Blue Team (SOC, CIRT), and the difference between Internal SOCs and MSSPs.

## TL;DR

  * [cite_start]**Hierarchy:** The **CISO** (Chief Information Security Officer) leads the security strategy, reporting to executives (CEO)[cite: 981, 992].
  * **Blue Team:** The defensive side of security. [cite_start]Includes SOC (monitoring), CIRT (incident response), and specialized roles (Forensics, Threat Intel)[cite: 1007].
  * **SOC Structure:** **L1 Analysts** triage alerts; **L2 Analysts** investigate complex threats; **SOC Engineers** maintain tools; [cite_start]**SOC Managers** oversee operations[cite: 1014].
  * **Career Path:** L1 is the entry point. [cite_start]Progression leads to L2, Management, or specialized roles like Digital Forensics or AppSec[cite: 1046].
  * [cite_start]**MSSP vs. Internal:** MSSPs protect multiple clients (high pressure, broad tools), while Internal SOCs protect one company (calm, deep tool knowledge)[cite: 1040, 1043].

## Security Hierarchy & Decision Logic

The module focuses on mapping specific security incidents to the correct personnel. Below are the logical "commands" or decisions you make when routing tasks in a corporate environment.

### 1\. Alert Triage & Initial Response

**Role:** SOC L1 Analyst / SOC L2 Analyst
**Logic:** If it is a routine alert, send to L1. If it requires deep analysis (malware/phishing), send to L2.

```text
Scenario: SIEM created an alert about FW-NY-01 firewall brute-force.
Action:   Assign to SOC L1 Analyst (Lucas)
Reason:   Routine triage of incoming alerts is the L1's primary duty.

Scenario: The HR manager launched a phishing malware. Deep analysis required.
Action:   Assign to SOC L2 Analyst (Susan)
[cite_start]Reason:   L2s handle complex investigations and deep-dive analysis[cite: 1014].
```

### 2\. Infrastructure & Tooling

**Role:** SOC Engineer
**Logic:** If a security tool (SIEM, EDR, Firewall) is broken, full, or needs configuration, it is an engineering task.

```text
Scenario: The SIEM is unavailable due to a storage limit.
Action:   Assign to SOC Engineer (Eugen)
[cite_start]Reason:   Engineers configure and maintain the security tools[cite: 1014].
```

### 3\. Major Incident Response ("Firefighting")

**Role:** CERT/CIRT Lead (Incident Responder)
**Logic:** If the company is *actively* compromised (Ransomware, Data Breach) and it is out of control, call the firefighters.

```text
Scenario: The office in France was hit with ransomware. Immediate response required!
Action:   Assign to CERT Lead (Robert)
[cite_start]Reason:   CIRT handles breaches that go out of control or require emergency response[cite: 1016].
```

### 4\. Specialized & Offensive Tasks

**Roles:** GRC Auditor, Penetration Tester, Threat Researcher
**Logic:** Match the specific domain (Compliance, Offense, Intel) to the specialist.

```text
Scenario: Servers storing credit cards require PCI DSS audit.
Action:   Assign to GRC Auditor (Nick)
[cite_start]Reason:   GRC specialists manage compliance and regulations[cite: 999].

Scenario: Check the new version of tryhackme.thm for vulnerabilities.
Action:   Assign to Penetration Tester (Ben)
[cite_start]Reason:   Red Team/Pentesters look for security issues offensively[cite: 998].

Scenario: FIN7 threat group targets the company. Analyze their tactics.
Action:   Assign to Threat Researcher (Alice)
[cite_start]Reason:   Threat Intel Analysts gather data on emerging threat groups[cite: 1026].
```

## Career Paths: Internal SOC vs. MSSP

[cite_start]When choosing a job, understand the environment differences[cite: 1043, 1044].

| Feature | Internal SOC | MSSP (Managed Security Service Provider) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Client** | Protects **one** company (employer). | Protects **many** external clients (e.g., 60+). |
| **Pace** | Calmer shifts, focus on depth. | High pressure, urgent queues, fast-paced. |
| **Tools** | Deep knowledge of a **few** tools. | Broad knowledge of **many** diverse tools. |
| **Experience** | Learns from occasional major attacks. | Deals with attacks **weekly**; faster learning curve. |

## Gotchas & Pitfalls

  * **CISO vs. CEO:** The **CEO** focuses on *business* objectives. The **CISO** translates those into *security* programs. [cite_start]Don't assume the CEO knows technical security details[cite: 991, 992].
  * **SOC vs. IT:** In small companies, "IT" does everything. In large companies, "Blue Team/SOC" is distinct from IT. [cite_start]Do not assign a printer fix to a SOC Analyst[cite: 994, 996].
  * **L1 vs. Engineer:** L1 Analysts *use* the tools to find bad guys. Engineers *fix* the tools so Analysts can use them. [cite_start]If the SIEM is broken, the Analyst cannot fix it; the Engineer must[cite: 1014].
  * **Red vs. Blue:** Red Team attacks (offensive); Blue Team defends (defensive). [cite_start]They are separate departments under the CISO[cite: 998, 1001].
