# 52.SAL1 — Humans as Attack Vectors — Cheat Sheet

**Focus:** Understanding Social Engineering, Phishing, Deepfakes, and SOC Mitigation strategies.

## TL;DR

* 
**The Weakest Link:** Humans are often the "gatekeepers" that allow attackers in, bypassing technical defenses like firewalls.


* 
**Social Engineering:** Attacks that exploit human psychology (Trust, Urgency, Fear, Curiosity) rather than technical flaws.


* **Key Attacks:** Phishing, Malware downloads (via SEO poisoning), Deepfakes, and Impersonation.
* **Defense:** Requires a mix of technical tools (Anti-phishing, EDR) and human training (Security Awareness, "Trust but Verify").

---

## Attack Vectors (What to detect)

### 1. Phishing

**Concept:** Deceptive emails aimed at stealing credentials or access.


**Technique:** Sending an email saying "Account compromised" with a link to a fake login page.
**Goal:** Credential harvesting or malware delivery.

### 2. Malware Downloads & SEO Poisoning

**Concept:** Tricking users into installing malicious software voluntarily.


**Technique:** Using fake CAPTCHAs, malicious QR codes, or **SEO Poisoning** (making malicious sites appear at the top of search results).

### 3. Deepfakes & Impersonation

**Concept:** Pretending to be a trusted authority figure (CEO, IT Support).
**Technique:**

* 
**Deepfakes:** AI-generated video/audio (e.g., a "boss" asking for an urgent $25M wire transfer).


* 
**Impersonation:** Phone calls claiming to be IT support needing remote access for a "repair".



---

## Defense & Mitigation Strategies (The "Commands")

In this context, "commands" are strategic actions taken by the SOC Analyst or Security Administrator within the dashboard or corporate policy.

### Action 1: Deploy Technical Controls

**Command/Action:** Implement **Anti-phishing Solutions** and **EDR/Antivirus**.


**Explanation:** Tools that block malicious emails before they reach the inbox and prevent malware execution on endpoints.
**Example:** automatically moving emails with known bad domains to Spam.

### Action 2: Human Verification Protocol

**Command/Action:** Enforce **"Trust but Verify"**.


**Explanation:** Instruct employees to verify suspicious requests (especially urgent money/data requests) through a secondary channel (e.g., call the CEO directly if they get a strange email).
**Target:** Mitigates Deepfakes and Impersonation.

### Action 3: Education

**Command/Action:** Run **Security Awareness Training**.


**Explanation:** Teach employees how to spot phishing signs and reinforce this with simulated phishing campaigns.
**Target:** Reduces the success rate of Social Engineering.

## Gotchas & Pitfalls

* **Mitigation  Immunity:** No matter how good your tools are, some attacks *will* bypass them. The SOC must always be ready to detect what slips through.


* **The "Urgency" Trap:** Attacks often succeed because they make the victim feel they must act *now* (e.g., "Urgent business deal"). Train users to pause when they feel pressured.


* **AI Evolution:** Deepfakes are becoming harder to distinguish from reality. Old verification methods (like recognizing a voice) may no longer be sufficient.
