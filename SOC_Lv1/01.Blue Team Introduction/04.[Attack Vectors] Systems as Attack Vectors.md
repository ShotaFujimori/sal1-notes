# 53.SAL1 — Systems as Attack Vectors — Cheat Sheet

**Focus:** Understanding how attackers exploit vulnerable and misconfigured systems, and implementing SOC-level mitigation and detection strategies.

---

## TL;DR

* **Systems as Targets:** Unlike human-focused attacks, systems can be breached without user intervention through vulnerabilities, misconfigurations, and supply chain compromises.
* **Attack Categories:** Three primary vectors include Human-Led Attacks (weak passwords, malicious USB), Software Vulnerabilities (CVEs, zero-days), and Supply Chain Attacks (compromised libraries/updates).
* **Critical Distinction:** Vulnerabilities require patches from vendors, while misconfigurations require better setup and configuration management.
* **Defense Strategy:** Combine Mitigation (Patch Management, Network Protection, Training) with Detection (Vulnerability Scans, Penetration Testing, Configuration Audits).
* **SOC Role:** Although SOC analysts do not manage systems directly, understanding attack vectors and communicating risks to IT teams is essential for effective security operations.

---

## Attack Vectors (What to Detect)

### 1. Human-Led Attacks on Systems

**Concept:** Users inadvertently compromise systems through poor security practices.

**Techniques:**
- Inserting malicious USB drives found in public spaces
- Downloading malware from pirated software resources
- Reusing weak passwords across multiple systems

**Statistics:** 81% of breaches involve stolen or weak passwords.

**Goal:** Initial system access leading to data theft, ransomware deployment, or lateral movement.

---

### 2. Software Vulnerabilities

**Concept:** Security flaws in software that can be exploited to breach systems.

**Key Terms:**
- **CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures):** Public identifier assigned to discovered vulnerabilities
- **Zero-day:** Vulnerability discovered by attackers before vendors or security researchers
- **Patch:** Update supplied by software vendor to fix vulnerabilities

**Real-World Examples:**
- Shellshock: Linux vulnerability existing since 1992, discovered in 2014
- Over 40,000 vulnerabilities published in 2024
- More than 300 actively exploited in major attacks

**Notable CVE:**
- **CVE-2025-53770:** Critical SharePoint vulnerability dubbed "ToolShell"

---

### 3. Supply Chain Attacks

**Concept:** Threat actors breach trusted applications or libraries and push malicious updates to all users.

**Mechanism:** Modern applications depend on thousands of libraries. Compromising a single library affects all downstream users.

**Famous Examples:**
- **SolarWinds breach:** Affected thousands of companies globally
- **3CX breach:** Compromised VoIP software used by businesses
- **Lottie Player incident:** Even TryHackMe fell victim to this supply chain attack

**Challenge:** Difficult to protect against since organizations cannot always control all software present on systems.

---

### 4. Misconfigurations

**Concept:** Errors in system setup made by IT teams, often to simplify operations.

**Common Examples:**
- Using weak passwords like "1111" or "123456" for convenience
- Exposing databases to public internet without authentication
- Leaving default credentials unchanged
- Improper cloud configuration (AWS, Azure, GCP)

**Real-World Incidents:**
- McDonald's job application portal exposed 64 million chats due to "123456" password
- Misconfigured AWS cloud resulted in breach of 106 million bank customers
- Smart fridges improperly configured and used in botnet attacks

**Critical Difference:** Misconfigurations do NOT require software updates—they require better configuration practices.

## Defense & Mitigation Strategies

### Strategy 1: Patch Management

**Command/Action:** Implement systematic tracking and patching of vulnerable systems.

**Explanation:** Once a CVE is published, it becomes a race between attackers developing exploits and defenders applying patches. Patch management significantly reduces attack surface.

**Process:**
1. Monitor CVE databases (CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities Catalog)
2. Assess criticality and business impact
3. Test patches in staging environment
4. Deploy patches to production systems
5. Verify successful deployment

**Response Timeline:** Critical vulnerabilities should be patched within 24-48 hours when exploits are actively used in the wild.

---

### Strategy 2: Zero-Day Response

**Command/Action:** Implement temporary measures while waiting for vendor patches.

**Explanation:** For zero-day vulnerabilities, patches are not immediately available. SOC must implement interim protective measures.

**Temporary Mitigation Techniques:**
- Restrict system access to trusted IP addresses only
- Apply vendor-provided temporary workarounds
- Block known attack patterns on IPS (Intrusion Prevention System) or WAF (Web Application Firewall)
- Increase monitoring and alerting for exploitation attempts
- Isolate affected systems from critical networks

**Example Workflow:**
```
1. Zero-day discovered → Assess exposure
2. Apply IP restrictions: Configure firewall rules
3. Enable enhanced logging on affected systems
4. Monitor for IOCs (Indicators of Compromise)
5. Deploy patch when available
6. Remove temporary restrictions after verification
```

---

### Strategy 3: Proactive Security Measures

**Command/Action:** Implement regular security assessments to discover vulnerabilities before attackers do.

**Three Core Activities:**

**A. Penetration Testing**
- **Definition:** Authorized simulated cyber attacks to detect security flaws
- **Frequency:** Annually or after major infrastructure changes
- **Scope:** Can include social engineering, network attacks, application testing
- **Outcome:** Detailed report with discovered vulnerabilities and remediation recommendations

**B. Vulnerability Scans**
- **Definition:** Automated tools that periodically scan systems for known vulnerabilities
- **Tools:** Nessus, OpenVAS, Qualys, Rapid7
- **Detection Capabilities:**
  - Default passwords
  - Outdated software versions
  - Missing security patches
  - Known CVEs
  - Weak SSL/TLS configurations

**Common Scan Commands (Example with Nmap):**
```bash
# Basic vulnerability scan
nmap -sV --script vuln <target_ip>

# Check for specific CVEs
nmap --script vulners <target_ip>

# SSL/TLS vulnerability check
nmap --script ssl-enum-ciphers -p 443 <target_ip>
```

**C. Configuration Audits**
- **Definition:** Manual review of system configurations against security best practices
- **Standards:** CIS (Center for Internet Security) Benchmarks
- **Review Areas:**
  - User account permissions
  - Network service configurations
  - Firewall rules
  - Password policies
  - Logging and monitoring settings

---

### Strategy 4: Network Protection

**Command/Action:** Restrict system access to trusted sources only.

**Explanation:** Limiting network exposure makes systems significantly harder to breach.

**Implementation Techniques:**
- Network segmentation (VLANs, DMZ)
- Firewall rules restricting access by IP/subnet
- VPN requirements for remote access
- Zero-trust architecture principles

**Example Firewall Rule (iptables):**
```bash
# Allow SSH only from trusted management network
iptables -A INPUT -p tcp --dport 22 -s 10.10.10.0/24 -j ACCEPT
iptables -A INPUT -p tcp --dport 22 -j DROP

# Allow HTTPS from anywhere, but rate limit
iptables -A INPUT -p tcp --dport 443 -m limit --limit 25/minute -j ACCEPT
```

---

### Strategy 5: Antivirus and Endpoint Protection

**Command/Action:** Deploy antivirus and EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response) solutions.

**Explanation:** Similar to protection against human-targeted attacks, endpoint security can stop or detect many system-level attacks.

**Capabilities:**
- Signature-based malware detection
- Behavioral analysis for suspicious activity
- Real-time threat response
- Forensic data collection

---

### Strategy 6: IT Security Training

**Command/Action:** Educate IT teams on secure configuration practices.

**Explanation:** If IT personnel understand misconfiguration risks, they are less likely to leave systems unprotected.

**Training Topics:**
- Principle of least privilege
- Secure password management
- Change management procedures
- Secure baseline configurations
- Cloud security best practices (AWS, Azure, GCP)
- Common misconfiguration patterns and their consequences

## System Value and Impact Assessment

Understanding the value of different systems helps prioritize security efforts:

| Breached System | Attack Value/Impact |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| Personal laptop of school student | Steal gaming profiles, add to botnet (low business impact) |
| Bank IT administrator laptop | Access to internal banking systems (critical impact) |
| Criminal law firm mail server | Dump all mailboxes for blackmail (severe legal/reputational) |
| Industrial network core server | Deploy ransomware, encrypt entire network (catastrophic operational) |
| Government website management panel | Website defacement, activism (moderate reputational/political) |

**SOC Priority:** Focus detection and response efforts on systems with the highest potential impact to business operations and data confidentiality.

## Gotchas & Pitfalls

**Vulnerability vs. Misconfiguration Confusion:** Many SOC analysts attempt to patch their way out of misconfiguration issues. Remember that patches fix software flaws, not configuration errors. If the issue stems from weak passwords, exposed services, or improper access controls, no software update will resolve it—you need configuration changes.

**Zero-Day Blind Spot:** Traditional vulnerability scanners only detect known CVEs. Zero-day vulnerabilities will not appear in scan results, creating a dangerous false sense of security. This is why behavioral monitoring and anomaly detection are critical components of defense-in-depth strategy.

**Supply Chain Trust Assumption:** Organizations often trust updates from known vendors without verification. However, supply chain attacks exploit this exact trust relationship. Consider implementing update verification processes, staging environments for testing updates, and monitoring for unusual behavior after updates.

**Patch Fatigue and Delay:** With over 40,000 vulnerabilities published annually, IT teams often experience patch fatigue, leading to dangerous delays in applying critical updates. Prioritize patches based on:
- Active exploitation in the wild
- CVSS score and exploitability
- System criticality and exposure
- Availability of working exploits

**Configuration Drift:** Systems that start with secure configurations often drift toward insecurity over time as temporary changes become permanent and documentation falls out of sync with reality. Regular configuration audits are essential, not optional.

**The "Urgent Business Need" Trap:** Security shortcuts taken for urgent business requirements (opening firewall ports, weakening authentication) often remain in place long after the business need passes. Document all temporary security exceptions with expiration dates and automated reviews.

**Incomplete Vulnerability Remediation:** Applying a patch to production servers while leaving development and staging environments unpatched creates residual risk. Attackers can compromise less-protected environments and pivot to production systems.

## Key Statistics and Facts

- **81%** of breaches involve stolen or weak passwords
- **Over 40,000** software vulnerabilities published in 2024
- **More than 300** vulnerabilities actively exploited in major attacks
