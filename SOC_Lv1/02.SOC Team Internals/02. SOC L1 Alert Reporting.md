# 55.SAL1 — SOC L1 Alert Reporting — Cheat Sheet

**Focus:** Writing effective alert reports (The 5 Ws), knowing when/how to escalate to Level 2 (L2), and handling critical communication scenarios.

## TL;DR

* **Reporting:** You cannot just "close" an alert. You must document **Who, What, Where, When, and Why** (The 5 Ws) to provide context for L2 or future audits.
* **Escalation:** Escalate if it is a **True Positive** requiring remediation (e.g., isolating a host), a major incident, or if you are unsure. Never guess.
* **Communication:** If a communication channel (like Slack) is compromised, **do not use it** to verify the breach. Call the user. If you make a mistake, admit it immediately to L2.

---

## 1. Alert Reporting (The "Five Ws")

**Concept:** A standardized format to document your findings before escalating. This saves L2 time and creates a permanent record.

### Execution & Format

**Action:** Fill out the "Analyst Comment" or "Case Report" field in the SOAR/Ticketing system.

| Field | Question to Answer | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who** | Who is involved? (Attacker & Victim) | Sender: `support@microsoft.com` (Spoofed), Victim: `e.huffman`. |
| **What** | What happened? (Action/Event) | Phishing email received containing malicious attachment `REPORT.rar`. |
| **When** | Timestamp of activity? | March 27th, 2025 at 19:25. |
| **Where** | Which asset/IP/URL? | Endpoint: `Workstation-01`, IP: `203.0.113.45`. |
| **Why** | **Verdict reasoning**. | **True Positive**. SPF/DKIM failed, urgent language used ("600% increase"), known bad file type in context. |

**Concrete Example (The Phishing Report):**

> "True Positive. On Mar 27th at 19:25, user `e.huffman` received a phishing email from `support@microsoft.com`. The email failed SPF/DKIM checks (Who/Why). It contained a suspicious attachment `REPORT.rar` (What) and used urgency to trick the user. Recommended investigation for potential execution on the endpoint (Where)."

**Flag Obtained:** `THM{nice_attempt_faking_microsoft_support}`

## 2. Alert Escalation

**Concept:** Passing the ticket to a Senior Analyst (L2) when the scope exceeds L1 permissions or knowledge.

### Execution & Criteria

**Command/Action:** Reassign Ticket  Select L2 on Shift  Save.

**When to Escalate (Checklist):**

* [x] **Major Cyberattack:** Indications of APT, Ransomware, or Data Exfiltration.
* [x] **Remediation Required:** Host isolation, password resets, or firewall blocks (if L1 cannot do them).
* [x] **External Comm:** Needs involvement of Legal, PR, or Law Enforcement.
* [x] **Uncertainty:** You simply don't understand the alert (better to ask than guess).

**Lab Scenario:**

1. **Analyze:** Confirm the alert is a True Positive.
2. **Report:** Write the 5 Ws comment.
3. **Escalate:** Assign to **E.Fleming** (The L2 on shift).

**Flags Obtained:**

* Escalation: `THM{good_job_escalating_your_first_alert}`
* Webshell Alert: `THM{looks_like_webshell_via_old_exchange}`

## 3. Communication Protocols

**Concept:** How to handle edge cases where standard procedures might fail or be risky.

### Scenario: Compromised Chat (Slack/Teams)

**Risk:** If an attacker has compromised a user's Slack, sending a message "Did you log in?" alerts the attacker.
**Protocol:** **Out-of-Band Communication**.

* **Do:** Call the user on a mobile phone or walk to their desk.
* **Don't:** Message them on the compromised platform.

### Scenario: L2 is Unresponsive

**Risk:** Critical alert needs approval, but L2 is gone.
**Protocol:** **Chain of Command**.

1. Call/Ping L2 again.
2. Call L3 / Lead Analyst.
3. Call SOC Manager.

* *Note: Do NOT jump straight to the Manager unless L2/L3 are unresponsive.*

### Scenario: Broken Tools (SIEM)

**Risk:** Logs are missing or unsearchable.
**Protocol:** **Report & Document**.

* **Do:** Investigate what you can with available data. Report the broken tool to the SOC Engineer.
* **Don't:** Close the alert as "False Positive" just because logs are missing.

---

## Gotchas & Pitfalls

* **The "Silent" Error:** If you realize days later that you closed a malicious alert as False Positive, **tell L2 immediately**. Attackers can dwell for weeks; hiding your mistake aids the attacker.
* **Context Rot:** Logs in SIEMs often expire (3-12 months), but Tickets are kept forever. If you don't copy the relevant logs/context into the ticket *now*, that evidence may be gone *later*.
* **Blind Closing:** Never close an alert just because you don't understand it. That is exactly what the "Escalate" button is for.
