## TL;DR

* **NTFS is the default Windows file system** – it supports > 4 GB files, granular permissions, compression, encryption and Alternate Data Streams.
* **User Account Control (UAC)** runs admin sessions at low integrity and prompts for elevation, reducing malware impact.
* **Group Policy Objects (GPOs)** live in the *SYSVOL* share and refresh automatically (or instantly with `gpupdate /force`).
* **Kerberos (tickets/TGT → TGS) is the default AD auth**, while NetNTLM remains for legacy challenge-response.
* **Organisational Units + GPO = centralised configuration**; security groups grant resource access, not policy.
* **Trees, forests & trusts** let multiple domains (e.g., `uk.thm.local`, `us.thm.local`) co-exist and interoperate under one enterprise.

## Gotchas

* Deleting or moving anything in **`C:\Windows\System32`** can brick the OS.
* Running daily work as a local **Administrator** increases attack surface; use standard accounts plus UAC.
* **Disabling UAC or the Windows Firewall** is rarely justified outside labs.
* **Registry edits** apply instantly and lack an undo; export keys before tweaking.
* GPO changes may take up to 2 h to propagate—don’t assume instant effect without `gpupdate`.
* Trusts enable cross-domain logons but **do not** auto-grant resource access—ACLs/groups still apply.
