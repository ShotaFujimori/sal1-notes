# SAL1/Linux\_Fundamentals

tags: \[linux, fundamentals]

## TL;DR

* **Navigate & inspect**: `ls`, `cd`, `pwd`, and `cat` let you move around and peek inside files/directories; `find` and `grep` hunt through trees and logs quickly.
* **Create, move, delete**: `touch`, `mkdir`, `cp`, `mv`, `rm` (with `-R` for dirs) cover the full lifecycle of files/folders.
* **Shell operators**: `&` (background), `&&` (chain‐on‐success), `>` (overwrite redirect) and `>>` (append) super-charge one-liners and automation.
* **Permissions & processes**: `ls -l` reveals rwx bits; `ps`, `top`, `kill`, and `systemctl` show/shape what’s running and start services at boot.
* **Remote & transfer**: `ssh` gives encrypted shells, `scp` copies files across hosts, and `wget`/`python -m http.server` make quick pulls/pushes.

## Gotchas

* `rm -R` is irreversible—double-check path before execution.
* `>` overwrites a file; use `>>` when you just need to append.
* Hidden files start with a dot; they only appear with `ls -a`.
* Background jobs (`command &`) won’t show output until you bring them back with `fg`.
* Touch makes an empty file—remember to populate it with `nano`, `vim`, or `echo >>`.
