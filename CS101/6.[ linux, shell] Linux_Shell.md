## TL;DR

* Most distros default to **Bash**; check with `echo $SHELL` and list shells via `/etc/shells`.
* Daily flow: navigate (`pwd`, `cd`), list (`ls`), view (`cat`), and search with **`grep`**.
* Shell scripts: shebang `#!/bin/bash` + execute bit (`chmod +x`) + run with `./script.sh`.
* Core building blocks: **variables**, **loops (`for …; do …; done`)**, **conditionals (`if …; then …; fi`)**, **comments (`# …`)**.
* **Added Priv column** to surface **User/Admin** at a glance; rows alphabetised and the former blank column removed.

## Gotchas

* **Current dir isn’t in `$PATH`** on many distros—run scripts as `./script.sh`.
* **Shebang matters** (`#!/bin/bash`) so the intended interpreter runs your script.
* `chsh -s` may require re-login and can be policy-restricted by admins.
* `grep` on protected paths (e.g., `/var/log/*`) may require `sudo` or group membership.
* Wildcards like `{1..3}` and `*` expand in the shell—quote them when you mean literal text.
